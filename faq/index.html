Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about ATS, scores and your data.

The basics

What is an ATS?

An applicant tracking system is software that companies use to collect, store and sort job applications. Different companies use different systems, for example Workday, Greenhouse or Lever, and each one works a little differently.

Does an ATS automatically reject my CV?

Often it does not. Many systems read your CV into fields such as name, jobs and dates, and recruiters then search and filter. Some employers also add knock-out questions or filters. So a readable CV with the right wording matters, but nobody can promise what a particular company's system will do.

Does a high score guarantee an interview?

No. Scores help you compare roles and find gaps. They do not predict whether an employer will reply, and any score, from a person or a tool, tends to be optimistic.

Scores and results

What does the ATS readiness score mean?

It is a checklist of things that make a CV easy to read for automated systems: standard section headings, contact details, a clear layout, numbers and results, action verbs, sensible length, and a file that can be read. It is not any company's real score.

How is the job match calculated?

The quick check finds the important terms in the posting, giving more weight to requirements than to general text, and looks for them in your CV. The optional AI analysis goes further: it judges each requirement as full, partial or none, and then fixed rules calculate the score.

Why does one missing requirement lower my score so much?

Some requirements work as filters, such as a minimum number of years in a specific kind of work, a certification, a language or a specific industry. If you miss one of these, strong matches elsewhere rarely make up for it, so the score is capped and the page shows which requirement caused it.

Why can the keyword match and the AI score differ?

The keyword match only compares words. The AI reads for meaning, for example that "led a team" can count as management experience, and it also takes seniority and level into account. When they differ, the AI analysis is usually the more complete picture.

Should I copy keywords from the posting?

Only when they truthfully describe your experience. Use the posting's wording where it is accurate, and never add skills or tools you do not have. Interviewers will ask about them.

How accurate are the salary estimates?

When the posting states a salary, we read it directly. When it does not, the estimate comes from an AI that searches the web, and it is shown with a confidence level and its sources. Treat it as a rough assumption, not an offer.

Your files and data

Should I upload a PDF or a DOCX file?

Either is usually fine, as long as it contains real text. Avoid scanned or image-only files, tables, multiple columns, text boxes and icons. After you upload, the page shows the text a parser would see, so you can check it.

Is my CV stored?

The basic checks run in your browser. Your CV stays in the open browser tab and is forgotten when you close it, unless you choose to remember it on your device. The optional AI analysis sends your CV and the posting text to an AI provider so it can produce the result. Saved jobs and applications stay in your browser.

Why can I not see my saved jobs on another device?

They are stored in the browser you used. Use Download backup on the Applications page and restore the file on the other device. Accounts that sync automatically may come later.

What happens if I clear my browser data?

Your saved jobs and applications are deleted with it. Download a backup now and then to be safe.
