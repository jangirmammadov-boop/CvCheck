Why good CVs get ignored

Most of these problems have nothing to do with how good you are. They are about how your CV is read, and how you spend your time.

Your CV looks great, but the parser reads it wrong

Columns, tables, icons and text boxes can scramble the order, so dates and job titles land in the wrong fields.

CVcheck reads your file the way a parser would, and flags columns, tables, icons and scanned pages.

The posting asks for what you did, in different words

Recruiters often search for exact terms. "Managed access reviews" and "access recertification" can be the same work, but a keyword search sees two different things.

You see which terms are missing, and an optional AI analysis judges by meaning. You decide what is true for you.

Must-haves hide in the task list

Many postings name tools in the tasks that never appear under requirements, so a quick read misses them.

CVcheck reads the whole posting and points out tools that appear only in the tasks.

You cannot tell which roles are worth your time

Applying to everything is slow, and one missing hard requirement can matter more than ten matched skills.

Scores are capped when a hard requirement or a seniority gap is missing, and your saved jobs are ranked.

Applications disappear into a void

After a few weeks it is hard to remember where you applied, which CV you sent, and when to follow up.

The tracker keeps statuses, follow-up dates, the CV version and notes, and shows how many applications get a reply.

Salary is a mystery

Many postings do not show a salary, so you find out late whether the role fits your needs.

CVcheck reads the salary when it is stated. When it is not, you get a clearly labeled estimate with its sources.

What it cannot do

It cannot see the rules of a specific company's system, because companies use different tools and keep their rules private.

A high score does not promise an interview. Use scores to compare roles and find gaps, not to predict offers.

Common questions

Does an ATS automatically reject my CV?

Often it does not. Many systems read your CV into fields and let recruiters search and filter. Some employers also add knock-out questions. A readable CV with the right wording helps in both cases.

Is my CV stored?

The basic checks run in your browser. The optional AI analysis sends your CV and the posting text to an AI provider to produce the result. Saved jobs and applications stay in your browser.

Should I copy keywords from the posting?

Only when they truthfully describe your experience. Use the posting's wording where it is accurate, and never add skills you do not have.
